Training alone does not get young people hired

Young people in Gaza need market-aligned technical education that ends in real work. And the environment around them makes sustained learning hard.

Training is not the final product. Employment, income, university readiness and economic participation are the outcomes.

Barriers the village removes

  • Transportation difficulties
  • Unreliable electricity
  • Unreliable internet access
  • Few professional learning environments
  • Weak links between education and employers

An existing building on a site with room to grow

30,000 m²long-term site, about 30 dunums
~3,700 m²built-up area over three floors

The existing building (about 25 × 50 m) houses training, AI and software labs, coworking, career services, employer engagement, events and administration. Student housing is planned separately in Phase II.

Three phases, each unlocked by the one before

The village is not built all at once. Each phase opens only after it meets operational, regulatory, financial and market milestones.

Phase I · Validation

Academy launch

Lease the existing building and about 3 dunums. Launch with 200 students, prove the model, obtain licenses.

Phase II · Scale

Residential smart campus

Expand to the full ~30-dunum site, add student housing and services, grow toward 1,500 students.

Phase III · Vertical integration

International school

An English-medium secondary school that feeds the academy and prepares students for university.

Phase I: a 200-student employment academy

An employment academy, not a traditional training center. No housing yet, just the existing building and about 3 dunums around it.

Technical pathways, driven by employer demand

Software engineeringArtificial intelligenceData scienceCybersecurityUI/UXDigital marketingMultimediaDevOps & cloudAI agents & automationEntrepreneurship

Every track adds English for work, communication, freelancing, AI tools, project management, portfolio building and interview preparation.

Phase I is validated when it shows

  • 200 enrolled students, or an agreed minimum
  • Required operating licenses
  • Strong attendance and completion
  • Employer partnerships and placements
  • Willingness to pay, or sponsors willing to fund
  • A validated cost per student and cost of acquisition
  • A pipeline strong enough to justify expansion

The licensing authority, accreditation, fees and timelines must be formally verified before any public claim of accreditation.

Stage gates

  1. Gate 1 · Launch Phase ILease agreed, legal entity confirmed, fit-out funded, licensing path, recruitment and employer plans.
  2. Gate 2 · Approve Phase IIEnrollment validated, licenses held, outcomes shown, long-term land deal, BOQ done, financing secured.
  3. Gate 3 · Approve Phase IIIAcademy stable, school demand studied, curriculum and accreditation set, financing and operator confirmed.
